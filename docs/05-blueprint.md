# The blueprint

What the games people already love have in common, and where WestX stands against
each point. Researched 10 November 2026. Figures are from the sources at the bottom;
where I found none, the table says so.

## The games

| Game | Evidence it works | What it does that we want |
|---|---|---|
| **Genshin Impact** | About $10 billion in player spending across all platforms by the end of 2025 (Niko Partners estimate); over $4 billion on mobile alone by December 2022 (Sensor Tower) | An open world where you swap between a party, and combat built on elements that react with each other. Guides call the reactions "the core mechanic" of its combat. |
| **Zenless Zone Zero** | No sales figure found | Squads of exactly three. Hits fill an enemy's Daze bar; a full bar stuns them; hitting a stunned enemy starts a chain attack that swaps through the squad. Teams are built as Stun, Support and Attack. |
| **Destiny 2** | No sales figure found | Three classes with instantly readable identities: sources describe the Titan as brutish, the Hunter as elusive and the Warlock as mystical. All three use the same elements, each in its own way. |
| **Borderlands** | 100 million units across the series (2K, November 2026) | Every element has a job: shock strips shields, corrosive melts armor, fire burns flesh. Each leaves a damage-over-time effect. |
| **Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot** | Over 8 million units (July 2024) | Anime fights that happen inside open zones you travel through. |
| **Dragon Ball: Sparking! Zero** | 3 million in 24 hours; over 5 million by February 2025 | Proof that fast arena anime combat sells on its own. |
| **Monster Hunter Wilds** | 10 million in its first month | Deep combat with very different kits, in a seamless world. |
| **Elden Ring** | 28.6 million by December 2024 | An open world built around demanding combat and build variety. |
| **Breath of the Wild** | No sales figure searched | Terrain made of hills and peaks that pull the eye to the next point of interest, with those points placed in sight of each other. |

## Seven rules they share

1. **Three roles that need each other.** One sets up, one controls, one finishes. A
   class is defined by what it cannot do alone.
2. **Conditions that react.** Putting two different effects on one target makes a
   third, bigger thing happen. This is what turns three characters into a team.
3. **Every condition has a job.** Not three colours of damage: one wears down, one
   stops, one amplifies.
4. **A bar that breaks into a payoff.** Pressure builds visibly, then cashes out in a
   moment everyone can see coming.
5. **Swap, don't choose.** You bring the whole party and change who you are mid-fight.
6. **Many enemies, not one.** Area attacks and conditions only pay off against groups.
7. **A world that points at the next fight.** You should always be able to see
   somewhere worth going, and one region should be finished before a second begins.

One caution from the research: Final Fantasy VII Rebirth's party-synergy system drew
mixed reactions, with some players calling it bloat. One reaction that everyone
understands beats ten that need a chart.

## Where WestX stands

| Rule | Status | In WestX |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Three roles | **Built** | The witch owns the ground with area attacks. The engineer owns conditions. The lawman is the balance: solid everywhere, no setup needed. |
| 2. Conditions react | **Built** | Two different conditions on one fighter burn off together in an **overload**. |
| 3. Each has a job | **Built** | **Poison** wears health down. **Shock** stops a fighter, slows them and holds their dashes. **Hex** makes every hit hurt more. |
| 4. A bar that breaks | **Partly** | Guards break under pressure, and the lawman's thunderclap goes through them. There is no team payoff after the break yet. |
| 5. Swap | **Not built** | Each duel is one class against one class. |
| 6. Many enemies | **Not built** | Every fight is one-on-one, which is the worst case for an area-attack class. |
| 7. The world | **Not built** | One open arena. |

Rules 5, 6 and 7 are the gap between this prototype and the games in the table.

## What to build next, in order

1. **Group fights.** A gang of weak enemies around one strong one. This is the rule
   the three classes are most starved of: the witch's circles and the engineer's gas
   are built for crowds and have only ever had one target.
2. **The posse.** All three classes in one fight, swapping between them, so one
   character's condition can be another's overload.
3. **The first region.** One canyon and one town, with bounties that send you to
   those fights.
